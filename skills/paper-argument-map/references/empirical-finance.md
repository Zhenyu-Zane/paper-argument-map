# Empirical finance / economics reading decisions

Separate the economic phenomenon, the empirical association, and the author's causal interpretation. An introduction frequently announces results and identification without presenting their evidence; mark their provenance accordingly.

Useful questions, only when relevant:

- Which observation or theoretical disagreement motivates the research question?
- Is the claimed gap about missing data, population, mechanism, measurement, or identification? Preserve the author's scope; do not assert that all prior research lacks something based on one paper's positioning.
- What variation identifies the estimate according to the authors? Do not automatically label a policy change exogenous or a fixed-effects regression causal.
- Which assumptions are explicitly stated? Which additional conditions does the reader infer are needed? Put inferred conditions in secondary nodes with rationales, not in the author's mouth.
- Are mechanism tests, heterogeneity results, and robustness checks being conflated with direct proof? A mediation analysis alone does not establish a causal mechanism.
- Does the finding concern a particular sample, period, or proxy? Preserve these restrictions.
- Are contribution claims statements from the introduction or conclusions independently verified against methods/results?

Use `not_in_input` for a question such as "The supplied introduction does not state how parallel trends is assessed." Do not convert this into "The study violates parallel trends." Never infer that an unmentioned assumption was not considered elsewhere.
